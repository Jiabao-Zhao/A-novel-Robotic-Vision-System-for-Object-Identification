[Task]
Determine whether the candidate object belongs to the same visual product
family as the described CAD object, not whether it is the exact size variant.

[Target family descriptions]
{descriptions}

[Comparison rules]
1. Prioritize distinctive structural features over generic similarities
   such as color, a central hole, or basic rectangular or circular shape.
   Generic cues alone do not establish the product family.
2. Give A only when visible evidence positively supports the target family.
   Objects within the same family may differ in physical size. Large,
   medium and small gears are one family; M12 and M16 hex nuts are one
   family; 8, 12 and 16 mm round pins are one family. Do not distinguish
   these size variants using apparent crop size or infer physical dimensions
   from individually resized crops.
3. If only generic evidence is visible and distinctive family characteristics
   cannot be assessed, return C. Do not invent hidden features.
4. Return B only when clearly visible structural evidence contradicts the
   target family. A feature hidden by viewpoint or occlusion is not evidence
   that the feature is absent. Treat absence as a contradiction only when
   the relevant surface is clearly visible at sufficient detail.
5. Allow changes in viewpoint, lighting, shadows, reflections and partial
   occlusion. All described features need not be visible at once. Color is
   supporting evidence, not a substitute for structural evidence; color
   differences alone are not grounds for B.
6. Evaluate the candidate object, not the background, nearby objects or
   cast shadows. Different faces may look brighter or darker due to lighting.

[Decision labels]
A = FAMILY MATCH SUPPORTED
B = FAMILY MISMATCH SUPPORTED
C = INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

[Output]
Return exactly A, B, or C.
