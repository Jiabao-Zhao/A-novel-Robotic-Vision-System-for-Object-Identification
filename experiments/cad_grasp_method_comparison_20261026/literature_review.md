# CAD grasp generation: primary-source review

Reviewed 2026-10-26 for the local NIST task-board experiment. This is a source review, not a claim that these complete packages were executed here. The experiment report must distinguish upstream execution, an adapter around upstream functions, a reimplementation, and our added checks.

The strongest next comparisons are **GPG** for a clean geometric baseline, **F-GPG** for an assembly-specific geometric generator, and **Fabrica's grasp stage** for a modern mesh/Panda implementation. **EmbodiChain GraspKit** is the easiest match to the installed Open3D/Torch stack and is being examined separately in the comparison. None of the reviewed generator releases establishes success on our complete fourteen-part NIST set.

## Especially relevant research, without a verified drop-in release

**Kleeberger et al., [Automatic Grasp Pose Generation for Parallel Jaw Grippers](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.11660v1), IAS 2021.** This is the closest industrial-shape match: the paper illustrates a gear shaft, ring screw, U-bolt, and gear, and reports physical ring-screw bin picking. It filters surface point pairs using finger travel, friction, and a simplified cuboid collision model. Two-stage PAM k-medoids selects actual candidate poses with diverse positions and closing directions. This directly supports a small varied set rather than one universal count. However, it is explicitly offline: clustering can take minutes to hours. No released generator repository was verified in this review; do not label a local implementation as their original software.

**[Pickalo](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04690v1), 2026.** Its square/cylindrical/complex metallic components are relevant industrial evidence. Its CAD grasp stage cites Kleeberger: normal-ray pairs, an outward approach orthogonal to the closing axis, and gripper-mesh filtering. Grasps are generated offline and may be manually refined. Runtime uses object pose and weighted vertical-alignment/wrist-yaw/confidence/height ranking, then checks a top fraction. Its full perception system uses trained models. The project does not expose a verified generator release, and offline manual curation does not meet our runtime-generation preference. [Project](https://mesh-iit.github.io/project-jl2-camozzi/).

**Zhao et al., [Towards Robotic Assembly by Predicting Robust, Precise and Task-oriented Grasps](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v155/zhao21c.html), CoRL 2020 proceedings published 2021.** Gear-on-peg experiments make this task-relevant, but its three cascaded networks are trained for robustness, precision, and task suitability. It is a useful contrast, not a training-free replacement.

## What the shared-contact check does and where it came from

The local exact intersection of projected finite-pad polygons in a 25 micrometer contact band is an **added local validation rule**. It must not be attributed wholesale to Wan, GPG, or these other packages.

There is a related upstream check in [GPG's antipodal.cpp](https://github.com/atenpas/gpg/blob/master/src/gpg/antipodal.cpp): samples close to each side and within a 5 degree normal cone are collected; the common bounds in the two transverse directions define a shared rectangle; at least twelve samples from each side must lie in it. [Its caller](https://github.com/atenpas/gpg/blob/master/src/gpg/grasp_set.cpp) supplies a 3 mm band. Our 25 micrometer band is 120 times narrower, and exact polygon intersection is stronger than bounding-interval overlap. This difference motivates an ablation; it does not by itself prove every restored grasp is physically reliable.

F-GPG uses an opposing-normal tolerance and a gripper/mesh feasibility test, rather than this exact shared-polygon rule. Fabrica's inspected leaf uses an opposing-normal dot-product threshold and contact clearance. Dex-Net uses point contacts and friction/force-closure conditions. These models make different approximations; absence of our check is not a guarantee that their fingers fit a gear tooth, thread, or stepped connector.

Fabrica also has a separate [assembly-sequence optimization implementation](https://github.com/yunshengtian/Fabrica/blob/main/planning/run_seq_opt.py). It compares a lexicographic tuple: first favor supported holding, then fewer grasp changes, then a smaller moving-part torque proxy, then a larger relative-hand-angle/contact-count score. The torque proxy uses CAD contact locations, center of mass, and normalized disassembly direction. It is not an observed in-hand error or a certified precision measure. This is prior art for task-specific scoring, not a component proposed for our current pre-VLM candidate comparison.

## NIST evidence and practical interpretation

The [official NIST task-board page](https://www.nist.gov/el/intelligent-systems-division-73500/robotic-grasping-and-manipulation-assembly/assembly) explicitly covers pins, gears, nuts, and electrical connectors. [NIST-MOADv2](https://www.robot-manipulation.org/nist-moad) provides geometry and observations for 76 objects from boards 1–4; it is a dataset, not a released grasp planner. Its acquisition cameras do not imply that our runtime needs that camera arrangement.

[AssembleBench](https://github.com/hud-evals/assemble-bench) actually models fourteen ATB-1-style pin/gear/nut tasks, but is a VLA training/evaluation environment. It uses a Robotiq 2F-85, and its pegs start upright in presentation bores. Its successful pin demonstrations therefore do not establish that our Panda fingers can reach the same pin lying flat on our table. BNC connectors are not among its listed tasks.

For our experiment, preserve downward approach and target/table validation as the common contract. First compare released candidate generators under that same contract; then vary the local contact-band/normal/shared-region rules independently. Record candidate availability, rejection reasons, redundancy, and time before VLM selection. These are geometric tests, not measured assembly precision or verified pickup success.
